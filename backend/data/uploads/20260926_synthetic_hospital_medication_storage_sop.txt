HOSPITAL STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE (SOP): MEDICATION STORAGE AND HANDLING
Document Version: 2.4
Department: Pharmacy / Clinical Nursing
Effective Date: 2024-01-15

Section 1: Temperature and Climate Controlled Medication Storage Requirements
1. Room Temperature Storage: All medications labeled for room temperature storage must be maintained between 20 deg C to 25 deg C (68 deg F to 77 deg F), with temporary excursions permitted between 15 deg C to 30 deg C.
2. Refrigerated Medication Storage: Refrigerated pharmaceuticals (including regular insulin, biologicals, monoclonal antibodies, and vaccines) must be continuously maintained in medical-grade refrigerators between 2 deg C and 8 deg C (36 deg F to 46 deg F). Food or beverages are strictly prohibited in medication refrigerators.
3. Frozen Medication Storage: Frozen medications must be stored in specialized freezers maintained at -25 deg C to -10 deg C (-13 deg F to 14 deg F).
4. Continuous Temperature Logging: Digital data loggers (NIST-traceable) must record temperatures every 15 minutes. Manual physical log verification must be completed twice daily (08:00 and 20:00). Any temperature excursion exceeding 60 minutes requires immediate quarantine of affected medications and notification to Pharmacy Quality Control.

Section 2: Controlled Substances Storage and Security
1. All Schedule II, III, IV, and V controlled substances must be stored in a securely locked, double-keyed or electronic biometric safe bolted to the building structure or within an automated dispensing cabinet (ADC) with biometric user verification.
2. Physical inventory counts must be verified by two licensed clinicians at every nurse shift change. Discrepancies must be reported to the Pharmacy Director and Hospital Compliance Officer within 2 hours.

Section 3: High-Alert Medications and Look-Alike Sound-Alike (LASA) Segregation
1. High-alert medications (e.g., concentrated potassium chloride, neuromuscular blockers, IV heparin, concentrated opioid infusions) must not be stored on general patient floor stock.
2. Look-Alike Sound-Alike (LASA) medications must be stored in distinct physical bins with tall-man lettering and vibrant visual warning alerts to prevent picking and administration errors.

Section 4: Multi-Dose Vial Expiration and Open-Container Policy
1. Multi-dose vials must be dated and initialed immediately upon first puncture.
2. Unless specified shorter by the manufacturer, all opened multi-dose vials must be discarded within 28 days of initial puncture regardless of remaining volume.
